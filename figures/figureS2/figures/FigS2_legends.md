## Caption (paste into the Supporting Information)

**Figure S2.** Detailed motif preference analysis of m6A detection tools. (A) 5-mer sequence logos of the detected sites for the remaining 10 tools (per-tool mean KL divergence from the RRACH consensus is given above each column); the y-axis is the per-position information content in bits, computed on the replicate-equal-weight mean 5-mer probability matrix of each tool x species. (B) Heatmap of the top 5 preferred 5-mer motifs of each tool across four species; cell colour is the relative frequency of the 5-mer in that tool’s call set (shared scale, 0–0.55); bold 5-mers in dark blue match the RRACH consensus ^[AG][AG]AC[ACU]$.

## Panel A - KL divergence from the RRACH consensus

KL divergence (bits) between each tool's called-site 5-mer base frequency profile (positions -2..+2 around the centred A) and the theoretical RRACH consensus PWM (R = 0.5A/0.5G; A; C; H = 0.33A/0.34U/0.33C, as published). Profiles use full site-level counts of the cleaned callset export (callsets), are strand-normalised with the centre base A, and are averaged with equal weight over independent sequencing units (Arabidopsis n = 3, HeLa n = 3, mouse = the single mES_WT study; the two mouse studies are never merged). Zero-frequency bases use add-epsilon (Laplace) smoothing of both distributions, eps = 1e-4; the same computation underlies Figure 4 and the R3-6 analysis.

## Caveat (R3-6)

Motif profiles of called sites are dominated by tool-specific algorithmic properties: a two-way decomposition of the full-count KL assigns about 95% of the variance to the tool factor versus about 1% to the species factor, and the replicate-level variation is small (median CV about 5%). Panel A therefore characterises each tool's output rather than comparing biological motif usage between species, and cross-species motif differences should not be over-interpreted.
